Things to try

  • Find the bottleneck. Push demand to 8. Shelves go empty. Which bar is shortest? Fix only that one.
  • Waste money. Leave 1 forklift and add 6 trucks. Nothing improves: the trucks just queue at the dock. Capacity anywhere except the bottleneck is wasted.
  • Move the bottleneck. Add forklifts until trucks become the limit. Fixing one constraint always reveals the next.
  • Big trucks vs small trucks. Small trucks leave sooner but carry less. Big ones take longer to fill, so stores wait, then get a flood.
  • Run it tight. Set capacity just above demand. On paper it works. In practice shoppers arrive randomly, and shelves still run out now and then. That gap is why real networks carry spare stock and spare capacity.

The theory underneath

Theory of Constraints: a system's output equals the output of its tightest step. Here: output = the smallest of demand, forklift capacity and truck capacity.

Queueing: work piles up in front of the bottleneck (trucks waiting at docks) and starves everything after it (empty shelves).

Pull, not push: trucks here only load when a store is running low, so a healthy system shows idle forklifts. Idle time away from the bottleneck isn't waste. It's slack.
